# Week 5 failover demo: real processes, hard kill, manual promotion.
# Hardware: Ryzen 5 4600H. WAL on btrfs/LUKS/zstd:3 NVMe. Postgres via local socket.

## Setup
  omsd     -addr :19500 -wal <primary-wal>  -seed-accounts 4 -seed-symbols NABIL,ADBL
  follower -primary :19500 -wal <follower-wal>
  loadgen  -addr :19500 -workers 16 -duration 8s -accounts 4 -symbols NABIL,ADBL

## Load
  accepted 3714 orders, 2448 trades across 2 symbols

## Replication caught up (ReplicationStatus on the primary)
  ADBL : primaryPosition 1852, followerPosition 1852, recordsBehind 0
  NABIL: primaryPosition 1873, followerPosition 1873, recordsBehind 0

## Primary book before failover (GetBookSnapshot NABIL)
  logPosition 1873
  bids [496:1129, 495:2012]
  asks [501:45, 504:593, 505:1679]

## Failover
  kill -9 <primary>          # no graceful shutdown, no WAL close
  stop <follower>
  omsd -addr :19501 -wal <follower-wal>   # PROMOTION: a normal server, same log

## Promoted node book
  logPosition 1873
  bids [496:1129, 495:2012]
  asks [501:45, 504:593, 505:1679]
  -> depth JSON byte-identical to the lost primary's, at the same position.

## Promoted node accepts new business, continuing the log
  PlaceOrder NABIL buy 7@400 -> orderId 1874, logPosition 1874

## Journal after the whole exercise
  2451 trades, 0 imbalanced groups, net cash 0

## Why promotion is this cheap
  The follower stores records under the PRIMARY's positions, in the primary's own
  format. So its log IS a primary's log: promotion is pointing `omsd -wal` at the
  directory, with no conversion step to get wrong while the venue is down. It is
  also why the follower needs no separate checkpoint file -- its log's last
  position is its checkpoint.

## The honest gap (see ADR-006)
  Trades the old primary executed but had not yet settled are NOT recovered by
  promotion. Replay rebuilds the book, not the journal, so those executions exist
  in the log and never reach Postgres. Nothing above hit that window because the
  load had drained before the kill; a kill mid-flight would.
